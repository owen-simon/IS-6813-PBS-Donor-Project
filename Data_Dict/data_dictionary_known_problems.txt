# Professor's Notes: Data Quality and Cleaning

## 1. Data Dictionary Update

The professor identified an omission in the latest version of the data dictionary's **Known Issues** section.

Some ID fields contain synthetic labels, including:

- `SYN-DEF-`
- `SYN-UNMATCHED`
- `SYN-ORPHAN`

These labels identify artificially introduced data problems and will not be available in PBS Utah's real data.

**Important:** Do not use these labels as shortcuts for data cleaning or as predictors in modeling.

## 2. Known Data Issues: Copy Rows

Some records are copies of existing rows within the same file. These copies have either a missing link or an amount set to zero.

None of these copied records represents actual constituent giving.

### A. `unite_payments.csv`

- The donor or marketing code may be `SYN-ORPHAN`.
- The payment amount may be zero.
- A copied row shares the `opportunity_id` of the original payment.
- Synthetic copy IDs begin with `SYN-DEF-`.

### B. `soft_credits.csv`

- The recipient may be `SYN-ORPHAN`.
- The amount may be zero.
- Synthetic copy IDs begin with `SYN-DEF-`.

### C. `team_approach_legacy_payments.csv`

- Constituent IDs may be blank.
- A copied row shares the `source_code` of the original record.
- Synthetic copy IDs begin with `SYN-UNMATCHED`.

## 3. Data Cleaning Requirements

Develop reusable data quality checks that identify problems using:

1. **Links:** Validate relationships between records and their associated constituents, payments, or marketing codes.
2. **Amounts:** Examine zero amounts and other potentially invalid payment values.
3. **Dates:** Check for missing, inconsistent, or invalid dates.
4. **Duplicates:** Identify copied or duplicated records using relevant fields and relationships.

Do not rely on synthetic labels to identify problematic records.

## 4. ID Handling Requirements

- Retain IDs for joining datasets and tracking records.
- Do not use IDs or synthetic labels as model predictors.
- Do not remove records solely because their IDs contain `SYN`.
- Evaluate records using data quality checks rather than ID prefixes.

## 5. Documentation Requirements

Document all data cleaning decisions, including:

- The data quality issue identified.
- The checks used to identify the issue.
- The decision to retain, correct, exclude, or flag affected records.
- Whether the decision depends on characteristics specific to the synthetic dataset.

**Important distinction:** In real PBS Utah data, a missing marketing-code match does not necessarily indicate that a payment is invalid.

## 6. Key Takeaways

- Identify data quality problems through reusable validation checks.
- Do not rely on synthetic identifiers or labels.
- Preserve IDs for joins and record tracking, but exclude them from modeling predictors.
- Distinguish between genuinely invalid records and records with missing relationships.
- Document all cleaning decisions and their applicability to real-world data.
